Every ROM looks fast in the first minute. Any build you flash onto a cold, freshly booted POCO X7 Pro will feel quick, because nothing has warmed up yet. So the question here was not "is Axion OS 2.4 fast", it was "does it stay fast when the phone is hot and the session is long".

That is a harder thing to test and a much more useful thing to know if you play games on your phone.

## What I tested

- **Antutu.** A full run, not a partial one, so the score means something.
- **Geekbench 6.** Single-core and multi-core, for a sense of how the chip behaves under short bursts.
- **PUBG Mobile.** Real matches, watching frame stability instead of one good moment.
- **Free Fire.** Settings pushed a little higher than I normally play.
- **COD Mobile.** The heaviest load and the longest session of the set.
- **Asphalt.** Graphics-heavy, which makes thermal throttling show up early.
- **Heating.** How the phone handled the load across the whole run.
- **Battery.** Gaming drain compared with ordinary use.

## Why one score tells you nothing

Benchmarks measure a short burst. Games run for half an hour. Those are not the same thing, and the gap between them is exactly where a build stops feeling good.

When a phone gets hot, the firmware pulls the performance back to protect it. That drop is the part you feel as stutter in a match. A build can score well and still fall apart in minute fifteen, and a build with a slightly lower score can hold its frames longer and feel better to actually play on.

So do not pick a ROM off a single number. Pick it off the tenth minute.

## How I ran it

I did the benchmarks and the games in one sitting and let the phone get properly warm in between. That is deliberate. A cold device tells you about the chip. A hot device tells you about the ROM's thermal behaviour, which is the part you live with.

I also kept the conditions the same between games so the comparison means something, and I watched for the moment the frames start sliding rather than the first clean minute.

## What actually decides this

Three things show up over and over when a custom ROM claims a gaming advantage:

- **Frame stability.** Not the peak number, the floor. Does it stay smooth when the phone is warm?
- **Thermals.** How quickly does the phone reach the point where it has to slow down, and how hard does it slow down?
- **Battery drain.** A build that runs cooler but drinks power is not a win for a long session.

A custom ROM can genuinely change how a device manages heat and CPU behaviour. It can also do nothing at all under load, no matter how the score looks on paper.
