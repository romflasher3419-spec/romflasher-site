If you have used OxygenOS on a OnePlus phone, you already know the personality. Clean lines, quick animations, and a layout that sits closer to stock Android than HyperOS does. On a POCO, that shows up the moment you unlock it. Same hardware, very different skin, and that contrast is the whole reason ports like this exist.

This is a first look. Not a review, and not a guide.

## Why this is a look and not an install guide

I do not have verified build notes for this port in front of me. No confirmed version number, no build date, no tested bug list and no download files I would sign my name to.

So I am not going to invent any of them. That is the rule on this channel: if a number is not in front of me, it does not go in the description, and it does not go in a step you might follow. A wrong file name in a flashing guide is worse than no guide at all.

What I can show you is what is actually on the screen.

## What it looks like

The port boots and runs on the POCO X7 Pro. What you notice first is the layout. Where HyperOS is busy, OxygenOS is calmer. The quick settings, the app drawer and the general spacing all read closer to stock Android, and the animations move like a OnePlus, not like a Xiaomi.

On a OnePlus that feels normal. On a POCO it feels new, because you keep waiting for the HyperOS touches that never arrive. If you came from a OnePlus and moved to this phone, that is probably the feeling you have been missing.

## What is different from HyperOS

- The visual language is lighter. Fewer custom pieces, less of the layered MIUI look.
- The animation feel is quicker and cleaner, closer to stock Android pacing.
- The settings layout follows the OxygenOS shape rather than Xiaomi's.

None of that is a benchmark. It is what a first look can actually tell you.

## What I am not claiming

I am not going to call this stable enough for a daily driver, because I have not tested it properly for that. Ports like this one can be perfectly usable or they can have an odd corner that only shows up after a week, and you cannot tell the difference from a short look.

I also cannot tell you how the camera behaves long term, whether certification holds up across days of use, or how the battery settles. Those things need time.

## What usually matters with a port like this

These are the general rules for an AOSP-style port on this device, not measurements from this build:

- **Clean flash, not dirty.** A port that runs on a different base will not thank you for a dirty flash.
- **The camera is usually the fiddly part.** Ports often need a workaround for the stock camera app, so do not expect it to be perfect on day one.
- **Check certification and integrity after first boot.** If the Play Store complains, that is the first thing to sort out.

Treat those as things to verify on your own phone, not promises.

## Who this is worth watching for

If you miss OxygenOS from a OnePlus and you want to see whether that feel belongs on the POCO X7 Pro, this is a good look at exactly that. Watch how the home screen is arranged, how the quick settings behave and whether the animations are the kind you would want on your own phone every day.

That is what a first look is for. Whether it belongs on your phone after a week of real use is a different question, and it deserves a real test rather than a short clip.
