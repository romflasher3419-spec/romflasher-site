I flashed Evolution X 11.5.3 on the POCO X7 Pro and then used the phone the way a
normal person does. Long gaming sessions, quick app switching, hours of scrolling,
and a warm afternoon of thermals. Nothing was cherry-picked for the first thirty
seconds, because the first thirty seconds never tell you anything.

The question I wanted answered is the boring one. Do the frames hold up after
twenty minutes, does the back of the phone turn into a hand warmer, and do the
animations still feel snappy once everything is hot? That last part matters more
than any first-run impression, because every build looks fast in its first five
minutes.

## What was tested

- Real gaming on this build, watched for frame stability instead of one lucky
  screenshot.
- Heating after a long session, and how quickly the phone came back down.
- Smoothness: scrolling, app opening and animation consistency.
- Touch response in game, because stutter and input lag are two different
  problems.
- Battery behaviour during gaming compared with lighter everyday use.
- Stability across the whole run, looking for crashes, freezes or odd reboots.
- Daily-driver feel once the novelty of a fresh flash wears off.

## What it looks like cold

Straight after a reboot, this build is quick and the interface feels light. Apps
open without a pause, the launcher slides around cleanly, and the animations have
that responsive feel that makes a phone pleasant to hold. If your sessions are
short, this is most of what you will ever see, and it is good.

That is expected. On a cool phone with nothing running in the background, almost
every performance-first build behaves this way.

## What changes when it gets warm

Here is the honest part. Keep a long session going and the phone starts managing
itself. Frames settle, the system gets more conservative, and the back of the phone
warms up. That is normal for this class of hardware, and it is exactly what the
heating section of the video shows rather than something that went wrong on this
particular build.

Whether that matters is entirely about how you play. Short bursts and you will
mostly notice how smooth it feels. Long matches, and the thermal behaviour is the
part you should watch before you decide.

## Smoothness and touch

Smoothness held up better than I expected once everything was warm. Scrolling
stayed consistent and app switching did not develop that half-second hitch that
tired builds get. Touch response in game is worth separating from the frame rate:
stutter and input lag are two different complaints, and here the input stayed
predictable even when the frame rate was being managed.

## Battery

Gaming drains faster than everyday use, and this build is no exception. Typical
gaming load pulls the battery down noticeably faster than scrolling and messaging
do. Nothing surprising, but if you game for hours away from a charger, plan for
it.

## The honest verdict

This is a performance-first build and it behaves like one. Cold, it is fast and
the UI feels light. Warm, the story is more honest: keep a long session going and
the phone manages itself, which is normal for this class of hardware and exactly
what the heating section shows. Short bursts feel smooth. Long matches should look
at the thermal part before you decide.

One thing to remember: this is an UNOFFICIAL build, so small bugs can show up
between releases. Flash it knowing that, and keep your previous setup backed up.

## Who should install it

Install it if you want a quick, light-feeling UI and your gaming happens in
shorter bursts. Think twice if your sessions run long and you are chasing
sustained performance rather than a fast first impression. Either way, it is an
unofficial build, so go in with your eyes open.
