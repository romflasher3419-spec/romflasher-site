## Why the order matters

Most reviews you read test one thing, screenshot it, and move on after the phone has cooled. That tells you what the hardware can do when it is fresh and comfortable.

Back-to-back testing tells you something different and more useful: what it does on your third match, when the room is warm and the phone has been working for an hour. Peak scores are marketing. Sustained behaviour is what you actually live with.

So the two questions the title poses get answered properly rather than teased:

- Does the Antutu run climb past a million?
- Does the CPU really throw up a temperature as dramatic as the title suggests?

Both get a real answer in the run, not a screenshot taken at the best possible moment.

## What sustainable really looks like

A build can look incredible in a single benchmark and feel ordinary by the time you are into your third match. That gap is exactly what this test is built to expose.

Play in short bursts and you will enjoy the headroom. Play for hours, and the throttle section matters most — because that is the part that decides how your evening actually goes. If a build holds up for two hours of gaming, it will handle a long day of normal use without you ever noticing the thermal behaviour at all.

## Reading the numbers honestly

A few things to keep in mind before you compare this run to anyone else's:

- **Room temperature changes the result.** A warm room throttles earlier than a cold one. Same phone, different score.
- **A case traps heat.** Benchmarks with a case on are not the same as benchmarks without one.
- **Setup matters.** Background sync, a live wallpaper, dozens of apps waking up — all of that eats into the headroom a benchmark is trying to measure.
- **One number is a data point, not a verdict.**

Treat the scores here the way you would treat any single run: useful for comparison if the conditions match, misleading if they do not.
