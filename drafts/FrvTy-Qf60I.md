Android 13 on the MI 11X feels like Android should. Clean, quick, no junk. EvolutionX takes the good parts of Pixel software and adds just enough of its own settings without burying you in menus. This build, v7.0, arrived as an official beta, so I did the first boot on camera to show what you actually get before you commit.

It is worth saying clearly: this is a beta. That is not a reason to avoid it, but it is a reason to know what you are signing up for.

## What it looks like

The setup wizard runs like any modern Android first boot. Once you are in, the Android 13 notification shade and quick settings layout are intact, so nothing feels like an older base wearing a new number. The theme engine and the customisation parts EvolutionX is known for are all present, and gesture navigation feels the way you would want it to.

The whole thing reads as Pixel-adjacent. That is the point of EvolutionX — familiar Google software with a few additions, rather than a completely new interface to learn.

## What I checked

- First boot time and how the setup wizard behaves.
- Whether the Android 13 notification and quick settings layout arrived intact.
- The theme engine and the parts EvolutionX is known for.
- Gesture navigation and the usual system smoothness.
- Whether the camera app opens and shoots, since that is where custom ROMs fall over.
- Basic stability over the first hours, because a beta is a beta.

That camera check matters more than it sounds. Camera quality on a custom ROM always depends on the maintainer, so test it yourself before you make this your only phone. If the camera is your main tool, shoot some real photos on a real subject before you trust it.

## What beta means here

- Features can change or vanish between builds.
- Some banking and DRM apps may detect an unlocked bootloader.
- Dirty flashing over an older build can cause bootloops. Clean flash when in doubt.

None of that is specific to EvolutionX. It is what an official beta means anywhere. If you want a ROM that never surprises you, a stable release is the other path.

## Who should install it

- You want a clean Android 13 feel on the MI 11X with Pixel-style software.
- You are comfortable with beta movement and can reflash when a build goes sideways.
- You keep a stable ROM zip nearby as a fallback.
- You test the camera yourself before relying on it.

If you need your phone to be bulletproof and never change under you, wait for the stable path. If you enjoy the process, a beta like this is a good way to live on current Android.

## If you want to flash it

1. Unlock the bootloader and install a compatible recovery.
2. Back up system and data first.
3. Wipe system, data, cache and dalvik.
4. Flash the ROM zip, then wipe cache and dalvik.
5. Reboot and let the first boot finish on its own.

Flash it the way the release post tells you when it comes to Gapps — do not assume. Some builds expect you to add them, and some do not. The release notes are the authority, not a forum comment.

If the driver side is the part that slows you down, the free Rom Flasher tool on GitHub bundles the ADB and fastboot driver install into a couple of clicks. It is free, and the link is in the downloads section.
