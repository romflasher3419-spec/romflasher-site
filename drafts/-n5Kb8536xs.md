::summary
You flashed an AOSP build, played with a modded MIUI, and now you want your phone
back to plain stock MIUI. No Mi Flash Tool, no drama. A fastboot ROM, platform
tools and one script are enough — as long as the files land in the right folder,
which is exactly where most people quietly give up.

::body
Let me guess how this went. You were on a custom ROM, things were fine for a
while, then one small thing started bothering you — a bank app refusing to open,
a camera that's a step behind stock, or just that itch to go back to something
that simply works. So now you are staring at a phone running something you want
gone, wondering if going back to stock means a weekend of Fastboot commands.

It does not. This method is short. It is also unforgiving about one single thing:
where the files sit. Get that right and the whole job is a double-click.

## What you actually need

- The **fastboot ROM** for your exact model. Not the recovery package — this
  method needs the fastboot one, and they are not the same download.
- **Platform Tools** (adb + fastboot) extracted on your PC.
- The **ADB and Fastboot driver** installed, so Windows actually sees the phone.
  If Device Manager shows an unknown device, stop here and fix that first.
- A cable that is not falling apart, and a battery above 50%.
- Twenty minutes where you can leave the phone plugged in and alone.

That fourth point sounds like a joke. It isn't. Most "this ROM bricked my phone"
stories are actually a cable that dropped mid-flash.

## Step 1 - take the fastboot package, not the recovery one

Go to the link below for your model and download the fastboot ROM. These packages
are big, usually a few gigabytes, and they contain the flash scripts plus the raw
images. If the file you downloaded ends up being a small recovery zip with an
`updater-script` inside and no `.bat` files, you picked the wrong one. Go back and
take the other one.

Match the model exactly. A ROM for another variant will flash happily and then
leave you with a phone that boots to a black screen, and that is a much longer
evening.

## Step 2 - extract it properly and drop the platform tools in

Extract the ROM zip into a folder you can find again, something like
`C:\miui\`. Use 7-Zip or WinRAR — not the built-in Windows extractor, which
sometimes flattens the structure and gives you a folder full of loose files.
If the extracted folder has no `flash_all.bat` inside, the extraction went wrong.

Now copy the **contents** of your platform tools folder — `adb.exe`,
`fastboot.exe` and the DLLs next to them — into that same ROM folder. They have
to sit beside the images and the script, not in a subfolder. This is the step
people skip, and it is exactly why the script opens a black window and vanishes
before you can read a single line.

## Step 2b - a quick sanity check before you commit

Open a command window in that folder and run:

`fastboot devices`

Nothing connected yet, so you should just get an empty line and no error. If it
says "fastboot is not recognized", the tools are in the wrong place. Fix that now
rather than halfway through a flash.

## Step 3 - fastboot mode, and make sure the PC sees the phone

Power the phone off. Hold **Volume Down + Power** until you see the fastboot
screen — usually the bunny logo with text, no MIUI. Plug it into the PC and run:

`fastboot devices`

This time it must print a long serial number and the word `fastboot`. No serial
number means the driver is missing, and no amount of retrying the script will fix
that. Sort the driver out first.

## Step 4 - run the script and then keep your hands off it

Inside the ROM folder you will see several files. The one you want is
`flash_all.bat`. Right-click and run it as administrator. A black window opens
and starts printing partition names: `super`, `boot`, `vbmeta`, and so on. Some
lines take a while. That is normal.

Do not unplug the cable. Do not close the window. Do not tap the phone to see if
it is alive. When the script is done the phone reboots on its own into a clean,
first-boot MIUI, and the setup wizard appears.

## Where this usually goes wrong

- **Wrong package.** A recovery ROM does not contain what this method needs.
  It has to be the fastboot build.
- **Broken folder structure.** Extract with 7-Zip or WinRAR so the subfolders and
  the `.bat` files stay where the script expects them.
- **A dead USB port or a flaky cable.** If the window starts and then dies at
  `super`, that is usually the connection, not the ROM.

## Things people ask before starting

A full fastboot flash resets the phone. Everything on internal storage goes,
so move your photos off first. The standard script leaves the bootloader
unlocked, so you can keep flashing later if you want. And no, you do not need
Mi Flash Tool, which is the whole point of doing it this way.

If the driver side of this feels like the annoying part, the free Rom Flasher
tool on GitHub bundles the ADB and fastboot driver install into a couple of
clicks so you are not hunting Device Manager. It is free, and the link is in the
downloads section.

One last thing. Do a full boot and finish the setup wizard before you start
restoring anything. If something is going to break, you want it breaking on an
empty phone, not on top of two hours of restored files.

::faq
Q: The script window opens and closes instantly. What is wrong?
A: The platform tools are not sitting in the ROM folder, or the folder structure
broke during extraction. Put `fastboot.exe` right next to `flash_all.bat`, not in a
subfolder, and extract with 7-Zip or WinRAR instead of the built-in Windows
extractor.

Q: Does this wipe my data?
A: Yes. A full fastboot flash writes the whole image, so internal storage is
reset. Copy your photos, downloads and anything you care about off the phone
before you start, and expect the setup wizard on first boot.

Q: Will the bootloader lock itself again?
A: No. The standard `flash_all.bat` leaves it unlocked, which is what you want if
you plan to flash again later. Only the `flash_all_lock.bat` script relocks it,
and you should avoid that one unless you are selling the phone.

Q: Do I need Mi Flash Tool for this?
A: No, and that is the reason this method exists. A fastboot ROM plus platform
tools in the right folder is enough. Mi Flash Tool adds its own driver and folder
requirements that cause more failures than they solve.

Q: Can I flash the fastboot ROM of another Xiaomi model on my phone?
A: Do not. Packages are built for a specific model and region. A mismatched
fastboot ROM can write partitions your phone cannot boot from, which turns a
five-minute job into an unbootable device with no recovery.
